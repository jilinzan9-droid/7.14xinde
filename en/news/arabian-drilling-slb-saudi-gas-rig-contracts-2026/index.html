Arabian Drilling's SLB Gas Rig Contracts Extend Saudi Fleet Commitments

Published by Pratt Oil · 15 October 2026
Original announcement: 2026-08-31

The 11-rig August contract and eight-rig September follow-up from Arabian Drilling, with a practical view of equipment support for Saudi gas drilling fleets.

Discuss a Parts RequirementView Industry News

Arabian Drilling announced a five-year contract with SLB on August 31, 2026, covering 11 land rigs for Gas LSTK operations. Its Saudi Exchange disclosure values the agreement at approximately SAR 3 billion and identifies the rigs as incumbent units from the previous project. The agreement was signed on August 30. Read the August disclosure.

A September 14 follow-up announced a separate five-year agreement for eight additional land rigs, to be redeployed from the available fleet. Revenue contribution was expected from the fourth quarter of 2026. The release gave a projected revenue timetable; it did not confirm that all eight rigs had started work. Read the September release.

Continued operation and redeployment are different support tasks

For maintenance and purchasing teams, the distinction between incumbent rigs and redeployed rigs helps separate ongoing fleet support from readiness work on returning units. Our procurement analysis is that each group deserves its own review: the installed fleet's actual maintenance requirements on one side, and each returning unit's inspection and readiness records on the other.

The contract announcements provide a business context for planning. They do not replace equipment records, reveal maintenance findings or specify which replacement parts must be ordered. A supplier should therefore respond to a defined equipment requirement, rather than assume that a rig contract translates into an immediate parts order.

Keep mud pump requirements tied to the individual rig

When reviewing a mud pump on a drilling rig, buyers can organize inquiries by pump model, assembly reference and part number. Connecting those details to a rig asset number makes the discussion easier to trace when several units share a maintenance campaign. Similar-looking components should still be matched against the relevant drawing or technical record.

The same approach keeps commercial comparisons meaningful. Buyers need to know whether quotations cover the same component, quantity and scope, and which technical points remain open. Fleet-level contract value alone cannot answer those questions.
