Halliburton's Aramco Award Sets Out Saudi Intelligent Fracturing Plans

Published by Pratt Oil · 15 October 2026
Original announcement: 2026-07-15

Halliburton's July 2026 Aramco award covers unconventional stimulation and completions. Read the announced automation plans and fluid-end parts implications.

Discuss a Parts RequirementView Industry News

Halliburton announced on July 15, 2026 that Aramco had awarded it a multi-year contract for integrated stimulation and completion services supporting unconventional gas development in Saudi Arabia. The company said deployment of OCTIV Auto Frac and Sensori fracturing monitoring services would begin in the third quarter of 2026. Its announcement also outlined plans for further local manufacturing investment, supply-chain development and workforce programs. Source: Halliburton's July 15 announcement.

Distinguish the announced plan from verified deployment

The release establishes the award and the deployment timetable announced at that time. This article has not independently verified whether the planned rollout has been completed. It also does not identify a pump model, installed fluid-end design or a spare parts order.

For pressure pumping maintenance teams, the relevant discussion is how to document mechanical components alongside changes to operating systems. The purchasing analysis below focuses on the boundary between operating systems and mechanical assemblies.

Keep automation records and mechanical records connected

An automation platform's name does not identify a compatible replacement fluid end. A buyer still needs the installed assembly reference, drawing revision and the configuration approved by the equipment owner. Treat the monitoring or control system and the pressure-containing assembly as related records with different technical boundaries.

When a workshop proposes a repair or replacement, its documentation should make that boundary clear. Does the scope cover a complete frac pump fluid end, a bare block or selected internal components? Which existing parts are intended to be retained? A clear answer helps the buyer compare proposals without mistaking different scopes for equivalent offers.

Use maintenance findings to define the purchasing scope

Inspection findings should drive repair decisions. If an existing assembly has damage or an uncertain service history, the responsible engineer should determine the required evaluation and acceptance criteria before a buyer orders individual parts. A catalogue match alone cannot establish fitness for continued service.

For regional workshops, this creates a practical documentation task: retain the inspection report, assembly identification and approved parts list together. Record substitutions and revisions so that the next maintenance team can reconstruct the equipment configuration.

Pratt Oil's fracturing pump parts page provides a starting point for reviewing component categories. Use the buyer's actual equipment records for the technical review of any proposed supply.
